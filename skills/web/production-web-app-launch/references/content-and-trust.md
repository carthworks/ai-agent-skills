# Content and trust

Covers legal pages, conversion paths, user guidance, and content quality. Read for every project.

## Legal, privacy, and trust

- Privacy policy exists and is reachable from somewhere users will find it.
- Terms of service where the product has accounts, payments, or user-generated content.
- Cookie or consent handling where legally required by the audience and the tracking actually in use.
- The data-collection description matches what the code actually collects. A policy that understates collection is worse than none.
- Contact or support route exists.
- Refund, cancellation, shipping, or subscription policies where money changes hands.
- No placeholder legal text and no unsupportable claims ("bank-grade encryption", "100% secure", fabricated certifications).

Legal requirements depend on jurisdiction and on what the product does. **Do not report legal compliance.** Report only whether the pages exist, whether they are reachable, and whether their content contradicts observed behavior. Anything beyond that is NEEDS HUMAN DECISION.

Never write policy text containing facts you do not have. A privacy policy naming a retention period you invented is a liability, not a placeholder.

## Conversion and calls to action

- Every important page has one obvious primary action.
- CTA wording names the outcome. "Get started" is weaker than "Create your first project" wherever the specific version is true.
- Navigation actually leads toward that action rather than dispersing attention.
- Forms tell the user what happens after submission — especially where the real response arrives by email or takes time.
- Success and failure states are visible and distinguishable.
- No dead ends: every terminal page offers a next step.

## FAQ and user guidance

- The questions a real prospective user would ask before committing are answered somewhere: pricing, what happens after signup, account behavior, cancellation, delivery, support, and known limitations.
- Empty states and first-run states explain what to do next rather than showing a blank panel.
- Onboarding does not assume knowledge the user has no way to have.

## Content quality

- No Lorem Ipsum.
- No user-visible TODO, FIXME, or internal ticket references.
- No fabricated testimonials, logos, customer counts, or review scores. If placeholders exist, flag them — do not replace them with different invented ones.
- No placeholder phone numbers, emails, or addresses.
- No dummy pricing left in a live pricing table.
- No developer language leaking into UI copy ("null", "undefined", "Object object", raw enum values).
- Terminology is consistent — one name per concept across nav, headings, buttons, and docs.
- Grammar and spelling are acceptable.
- Dates, currency, units, and contact details are consistent and match the stated audience.
- Empty states read as intentional design rather than as something failing to load.
